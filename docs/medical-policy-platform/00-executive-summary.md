# 0. Executive summary

**Purpose.** This document is the assessment and design for an enterprise medical policy platform ("medical policy database") for Point32Health: one system of record for medical policies, medical-benefit drug policies, prior-authorization (PA) requirements and their machine-readable criteria across every brand and line of business (Commercial, Medicare Advantage, SCO and One Care, MassHealth and Rhode Island Medicaid, Marketplace), feeding the public websites, the provider portals and Availity, the electronic prior authorization (ePA) platform for CMS-0057-F, the UM system, claims edits and delegated vendors. It is written to be used for a vendor assessment, a build decision, and as the first design for delivery. No code was produced.

**Why now.** Six consumers need the same policy content and there is no single source they can be reconciled against. The Prior Authorization API must be live on 1 January 2027 for Medicare Advantage and Medicaid lines and must carry the PA list and documentation requirements; decision timeframes and specific denial reasons have applied since January 2026; the public PA metrics list has been due since March 2026; Medicare Advantage internal criteria must be publicly accessible with an evidence summary; Massachusetts changed PA rules three times in eighteen months (most recently the June 2026 amendments prohibiting PA for a list of services); the provider portals are moving to Availity Essentials from late 2026; and CMS-0062-P proposes to bring medical-benefit drugs into the same ePA pipeline.

**What the platform must be.** Content as data, not documents: structured sections, per-LOB applicability and effective dates, code tables against a managed terminology store, a PA rule model evaluated by one service that answers identically for the public lookup, the portals, the CRD service and the UM system, digitized criteria compiled into Da Vinci DTR packages and CRD rules, a committee workflow with an immutable audit trail, scheduled publication with provider notice enforcement, an archive retrievable by date of service, and reconciliation reports against every consumer. The requirements catalogue (section 4) has 99 items (93 prioritised Must/Should/Could requirements and 6 explicit exclusions); the regulatory inventory (section 2) derives a further 55 traced requirements from the rules and maps them onto the catalogue.

**Technology.** Buildable on the stack the interoperability team already runs (Java 17, Spring Boot, HAPI FHIR, TIBCO, GitHub, enterprise SSO, the corporate CMS and CDN). Four justified additions: a relational database with JSON support (PostgreSQL or the enterprise standard), an embedded workflow engine (Flowable or Camunda), a terminology service (HAPI FHIR JPA loaded from CMS, CDC, FDA and the licensed AMA distribution), and a structured-editor front end. The CQL toolchain, Inferno test kits and the HL7 Burden Reduction reference implementation are added in phase 2 as compiler and conformance gates. Nothing else is required; a search engine, a headless CMS and AI drafting are optional and later.

**Vendor assessment.** Itiliti Health's core product (a structured PA-rules database with a public lookup and CRD/DTR/PAS outputs, paired with Onyx, the incumbent ePA vendor) is real and has been delivered for Blues plans. Its outcome claims (30/20/15%, 80% automation, "first in the nation") are vendor-only, recycled since 2020 and unverified; "full compliance" is portal-mediated, not EHR-integrated. Not evidenced anywhere: committee and approval workflow, per-LOB effective dating and archive, Medicare NCD/LCD precedence, MassHealth rules, drug policies, delegated-vendor routing, MHK integration, X12 278, PA metrics, and a SOC 2 or HITRUST attestation. The company has 13 to 25 staff, about $7M raised, a new CEO and CRO since July 2026, and only Blues and Minnesota Medicaid customers named. Cohere Policy Studio (December 2025) is the closest overlap with a larger balance sheet but is new and strongest inside Cohere's own UM engine. MCG Path and InterQual Exchange/Transparency deliver licensed content over FHIR and host custom policies but do not author them. No vendor covers the whole chain.

**Recommendation.** Option D, hybrid: build the system of record (governance, crosswalk, PA evaluation, publishing) on the existing stack; buy digitization labour for the first-wave backlog through a competitive proof-of-concept on ten real policies (Itiliti, Cohere, Availity, InterQual conversion), with content delivered into the plan's platform in open formats (FHIR Questionnaire, Library/CQL, ValueSet, decision tables) as a contract condition; deliver Onyx and MHK through adapters with nightly reconciliation. Do not select Itiliti on the lean; score it on the section 7 sheet alongside the alternatives and demand the proofs listed there.

**Roadmap.** Phase 0 (Oct to Nov 2026) decision and proof-of-concept. Phase 1 (Dec 2026 to Mar 2027) system of record, corpus migration, PA lookup and public pages, January 2027 PA list and the CY2026 metrics list generated from the platform. Phase 2 (Apr to Sep 2027) committees, criteria compiler, Onyx and MHK extracts with reconciliation, first 100 digitized policies. Phase 3 (Oct 2027 to Mar 2028) adaptive forms for licensed criteria, drug-policy readiness for CMS-0062, simulation, IRR, parity metadata, AI drafting.

**Caveats.** Research was done from a restricted environment: search-engine excerpts were available, primary-source pages were not, and the standards content was read from the HL7 GitHub sources. Every regulatory citation carries a confidence tag and section 2.11 lists the primary-source pulls Compliance should make before configuration. No earlier medical-policy assessment document was found in the repository or the artefact history; the closest prior work (the ePA workbench internals, the PAS conformance review and the ePA interoperability brief) was used for the organisational context.
